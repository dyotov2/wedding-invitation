# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Wedding guests and households invited by Ekaterina and Dimitar, including older relatives who may be more comfortable with printed invitations, QR codes, phone calls, WhatsApp, and Viber than email or account-based websites. Guests usually visit from a phone and need to understand and complete the invitation quickly.

## Product Purpose

Create a personal digital extension of Ekaterina and Dimitar's printed wedding invitation for their celebration on 20 June 2027 at Midalidare Estate in Bulgaria. Each private household link reveals only the invited people, supports a clear per-person RSVP, and can later be reused for meal choices. The public root has no code-entry form; short codes remain an internal compatibility and recovery credential. A practical admin view keeps digital and manually received replies together.

## Brand Personality

Tactile, tender, and cultivated. The experience should evoke an intimate early-summer celebration at a vineyard estate: warm daylight, blooming garden flowers, beautiful paper, and the sense that a personal invitation has been opened just for the guest.

## Anti-references

Avoid corporate event software, generic wedding-template ornament, overly pale low-contrast text, tiny script typography, crowded forms, email-first onboarding, accounts, passwords, technical language, and app-like dashboard chrome on the guest experience.

## Design Principles

1. Make every guest feel personally welcomed before asking for information.
2. Preserve the emotional quality of a printed invitation while making every action obvious on a phone.
3. Design for the least technical guest without making the experience feel clinical.
4. Separate the emotional invitation, the RSVP task, and the later meal-choice task into calm stages.
5. Keep manual phone, Viber, WhatsApp, and paper replies equal to online responses.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use large readable type, strong contrast, generous touch targets, visible focus states, plain English, reduced-motion support, and clear status feedback. Do not depend on color alone. Avoid dense multi-column layouts on phones and keep contact alternatives available at every decision point.
